Unit 1 · Notes on the neck

Movable chords & their root notes

Your open chords are stuck in one spot. Today you'll learn a shape with no open strings in it, so you can slide it anywhere on the neck. The note under your first finger tells you which chord you're playing, and you already know how to name it.

Why open chords can't move

Slide your whole G chord up two frets and it falls apart. The open strings don't move with your fingers, so they clash with everything else.

Take the open strings out and the problem goes away. A shape where every note is fretted is a movable shape. Slide it up or down and it plays the same kind of chord, just with a new name. The name comes from one note: the root, the lowest note in the shape.

The whole idea in one line Same shape, new fret, new chord. To name it, read the root: the note under your first finger on the low E or A string. You learned those notes in Lessons 1 to 3.

Shape 1: the power chord, root on the low E

The easiest movable chord is the power chord, written with a 5 (G5, A5). It's only three notes, and no happy or sad flavor to it. It's the chunky sound under a lot of country-rock and Southern rock. Here's G5:

  • First finger: low E string, 3rd fret (G, the root).
  • Ring finger: A string, 5th fret (D).
  • Pinky: D string, 5th fret (G again, an octave up).

Look at the first and third notes. That's the octave shape from Lesson 2: two strings up, two frets up. A power chord is just that octave with one more note tucked in the middle.

Now slide it. Keep the shape, move it up two frets so your first finger is on the 5th fret. Low E string, 5th fret is A, so this is A5. Up two more, 7th fret, is B5.

Shape 2: the same power chord, root on the A string

Move the exact same shape over one string. Now the root sits on the A string, and the A string notes name the chord. First finger on the A string, 3rd fret is C, so this is C5:

Slide it up two frets to the 5th fret: A string, 5th fret is D. That's D5.

Play G5 (root on low E, 3rd fret). Without changing your hand, hop over to the A string, 3rd fret. That's C5. Now slide up two frets to D5. Only strum the three strings the shape covers. Mute the rest by letting your fingers lightly touch them.

Every chord lives in two places

Since you can put the root on either string, every chord has two homes. Pick the one closest to where your hand already is.

  • G: low E string, fret 3, or A string, fret 10.
  • C: low E string, fret 8, or A string, fret 3.
  • D: low E string, fret 10, or A string, fret 5.

Sharps and flats work the same way. A chord sheet in the key of D will ask for F♯m. F is fret 1 on the low E, so F♯ is fret 2. That's where the root goes. A gospel song in B♭? B is fret 2 on the A string, so B♭ is fret 1.

The same idea with full barre chords

A barre chord is a full, six-string (or five-string) chord where your first finger lays flat across the strings, like a capo. You'll build up the hand strength for these over time, so don't fret if they buzz. What matters today is that they follow the exact same root rule:

  • F and G have their root on the low E string (frets 1 and 3). It's the shape of your open E chord, moved up, with your first finger playing the part the nut used to.
  • B♭ and C have their root on the A string (frets 1 and 3). It's your open A chord shape, moved up the same way.

Look closely: the bottom three notes of each barre chord are the power chord you just learned. Learn the power chord now, and the barre chord is just more fingers on top later.

Put it to work: G – C – D with power chords

Here's the three-chord country progression from Lesson 2, all in one spot on the neck. G5 on the low E (fret 3), C5 on the A string (fret 3), D5 on the A string (fret 5). Your hand barely moves.

Game: find the root

These are roots you'll actually need for chords. Use either the low E or the A string, whichever you find first.

Today's 5-minute practice

  1. Play G5 at fret 3, then slide it up two frets at a time: A5 (5), B5 (7), C♯5 (9). Say each root name out loud.
  2. G – C – D with power chords: G5 (low E, fret 3), C5 (A string, fret 3), D5 (A string, fret 5). Four strums each, around and around.
  3. Call-out game: have someone call a chord ("E flat!"). Find the root on the low E or A string, drop the power chord on it, and strum. Bonus point for finding both spots.
