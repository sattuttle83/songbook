Friday review: roots, recipe & G major at work

No new ideas today. You'll run back through this week's tools (sharps and flats, movable chord roots, the major scale recipe and G major), then put every one of them to work in one little G – C – G – D – G.

The week on one card

Five things you picked up this week
  • Sharps and flats: sharp = up one fret, flat = down one fret. A♯ and B♭ are the same note with two names.
  • Movable chords: a power chord or barre chord is named by its root, the note under your first finger on the low E or A string.
  • The recipe: W – W – H – W – W – W – H (on one string, 2-2-1-2-2-2-1 frets) builds any major scale.
  • G major: G A B C D E F♯ G. One sharp, F♯.
  • Use each letter once: that's why it's F♯ and not G♭.

Warm-up: name that note on E and A

Everything else today leans on knowing your two bass strings. Here are the naturals on both, so you can check yourself:

The three highlighted spots are the roots you'll use in a minute: G (low E, 3rd fret), C (A string, 3rd fret) and D (A string, 5th fret).

Now the game. This one mixes in sharps too, on both strings. Remember the slide-one-fret trick: find the natural next door, then go up one for a sharp.

Shoot for 7 out of 10. If a sharp stumps you, say the natural out loud first ("C is 3 on the A string, so C♯ is 4"), then tap.

Recipe check: hear it and feel it

Quick ear refresher. The major scale has exactly two half steps in it, and in G major they fall on B–C and F♯–G. Listen for the "tight" spots:

Play G major up the low E string (frets 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15) once. As you play, say "W" or "H" for each move instead of the note name. You should say H exactly twice: 7 to 8, and 14 to 15.

Put it to work, part 1: G – C – G – D – G with a walk

Here's a progression that runs a big share of country and gospel songs. Play it with your open chords, four strums each:

Now dress it up with the G major scale. Going from G to C, the scale has two notes in between: A and B. Pick them one at a time on the bass strings and you "walk" right into the C chord:

  • G: low E string, 3rd fret (the root of your G chord).
  • A: open A string.
  • B: A string, 2nd fret.
  • C: A string, 3rd fret (the root of your C chord).

That's not a trick, it's just the first four notes of the G major scale, landing on a chord root. We'll do a whole lesson on walk-ups later; today it's a taste of how the scale and the chords fit together.

Count "1, 2, 3, 4." Strum G on 1, then pick G, A, B one note per beat on 2, 3 and 4, and strum C right on the next "1." Go slow. It's fine if it's clunky the first dozen times.

Put it to work, part 2: slide the whole thing up a whole step

Now play the same progression with power chords from Lesson 4. The roots are the three spots from the warm-up:

Here's where the week comes together. Slide every chord up two frets, one whole step. Because the shapes are movable, the roots just move up a whole step too:

  • G up a whole step is A. (Low E, 5th fret.)
  • C up a whole step is D. (A string, 5th fret.)
  • D up a whole step is E. (A string, 7th fret.)

Same song, a little higher. You just changed key without thinking about it, using nothing but root notes and a whole step. That's exactly what the transpose button in the Songbook app does on paper, and we'll dig into it properly in Unit 3.

Today's 5-minute practice

  1. One round of the name-that-note game above (E and A strings, sharps included).
  2. G – C – G – D – G on open chords, four strums each, twice through.
  3. Same progression, but add the G-A-B walk into the C chord. Three times.
  4. Power chords: G5 – C5 – G5 – D5 – G5, then slide it all up two frets for A5 – D5 – A5 – E5 – A5. Have the kids say which one sounds "higher."
